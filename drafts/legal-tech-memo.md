# LegalTech Is Coming for the $950B Legal Industry

*The legal industry is one of the most bloated, slow-moving, and resistant-to-change sectors in the U.S. economy. Here's why that's about to change.*

---

The legal industry is one of the most bloated, slow-moving, and resistant-to-change sectors in the U.S. economy. With global corporate legal spend surpassing $950B annually — much of that tied up in repetitive workflows and outdated practices — LegalTech presents a compelling opportunity for venture-scale disruption.

LegalTech is about redefining what work needs an actual lawyer. Whether it's due diligence, contract review, litigation prep, or case management, legacy firms still rely heavily on human capital for tasks that can now be automated with generative AI.

Here's an example of an inefficiency (one of many). In the expert witness sector, doctors bill law firms for expensive, extensive hours that are wasted re-organizing multi-thousand page medical records. It's highly paid labor, wasted on administrative work — an area begging for automation and structured workflows. Human oversight is necessary, but the right tech stack could reduce this friction dramatically.

Platforms like Harvey, built on OpenAI's infrastructure, have demonstrated how large language models can dramatically accelerate legal research and document review. Ironclad, Everlaw, Spellbook, and Luminance are similarly tackling slices of the legal stack — from contracts to litigation discovery — but most are still tools *for* lawyers. The next wave of innovation will go further as these companies ride the wave of improving models, and even train their own.

## Where this is headed

The rise of autonomous legal agents: specialized AI systems that can independently complete legal tasks like redlining documents, managing IP filings, or even initiating small claims processes. These systems will support lawyers, but also increasingly serve non-lawyers and enterprises directly — a massive TAM expansion.

Legal infrastructure and processes are becoming invisible and embedded. Just as Stripe made payments a background API, LegalTech startups will make legal compliance and documentation feel seamless and self-executing.

## What I'd invest in

I'd look at vertical AI-native platforms targeting highly regulated, document-heavy sectors where legal work is repetitive, error-prone, and time-sensitive. These environments — where compliance is critical and workflows are still largely manual — create ideal conditions for agentic tools and embedded automation.

The key is finding domains with enough operational pain to drive adoption, but enough structure to enable reliable system design. I'd be more cautious with general-purpose legal copilots unless they show a defensible wedge or strong network effects.
